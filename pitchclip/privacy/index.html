PitchClip Privacy Policy

Effective November 3, 2026

PitchClip has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. What you record and type stays on your iPhone, and on nearby phones you let follow your game or join your team. The one exception is Coach: when you tap Coach on a clip, ask for a Game Review, or turn on Coach for your player's at bats, a few seconds of video (or, for some pitching cards, only body points measured on this phone) and a few details go to PitchClip's server to write coaching cards (see Coach below). Otherwise the developer receives nothing from the app unless you choose to send feedback or a bug report.

What stays on this phone

PitchClip keeps what it makes on this iPhone: the video of each pitch with game sound; each clip's details (game and opponent names, jersey numbers, any player names you type, pitch speeds, plate camera calls and tags); lineups and your team list (My team), with each player's throwing hand if you set it; each team's logo, age group and usual batting order; player photos; pitch counts and rest dates, including counts your team's other phones shared; Coach cards, Game Reviews, at bat summaries, the numbers from past summaries, your thumbs up or down and note on a card, the swing history built from them, the body points measured for Coach, and the record of Coach Credits used; your speed setups, and the location of each field you chose to remember; your settings; the names of nearby phones in the Game feed; and a diagnostic log, with iOS's crash and performance reports for PitchClip.

Clip videos, clip details and the diagnostic log are in PitchClip's folder, which you can also open in the Files app (On My iPhone, PitchClip). The log lists game and opponent names, jersey numbers, any player names you typed and the names of nearby phones, never video and never your location. The pitch list, Recently deleted clips, player photos, the team logo, Coach cards, Game Reviews, at bat summaries, body points and swing history are in PitchClip's private storage, which the Files app does not show. Exported clips and thumbnails sit in temporary storage that iOS can clear.

If you turn on Record the whole game (for testing accuracy) in Help, For the developer, PitchClip also keeps all the video and sound of that one game in PitchClip's folder (FullGame), with a list of the clips it saved. It is off unless you turn it on, turns off when the game ends, is left out of backups, and stays until you delete it in Storage.

Parent contacts you save for a player (a phone number or email, picked from Contacts or typed) stay on this iPhone in PitchClip's private storage. They leave it only when you export them (Settings, Export contacts) or use them to text clips.

The app never sends any of this to the developer or to a server, except what Coach sends (see Coach). The hidden Field tools internet speed test, for the developer, connects to speed.cloudflare.com only when someone runs it. It sends only test bytes, no video or personal data; Cloudflare sees the phone's IP address.

Backups

If iCloud Backup or a computer backup is on, iOS includes PitchClip's data in this iPhone's backup like any other app, including clip videos, the names in them and the locations of fields you remembered. Player photos, the team logo and temporary files are left out of backups. Backups are kept by Apple or on your computer, under Apple's privacy policy, and the developer cannot see them.

To leave PitchClip out of iCloud Backup, turn it off in the iCloud Backup settings for this iPhone (Settings app, your name, iCloud).

Lineup screenshots and photos

To fill in a lineup, PitchClip can read a GameChanger screenshot (one you pick, or the newest screenshot in your photo library if you allow PitchClip to see your photos) or a photo of a lineup card you take in the app. The text is read on this iPhone with Apple's built-in text recognition. The screenshot or photo is not saved, copied or sent anywhere. Only the jersey numbers and names you keep are saved.

Field locations

After you save a speed setup at a field, PitchClip can ask to remember that field. If you tap Remember and allow Location (While Using the App), PitchClip saves the field's location with its speed setup, so speed is ready by itself the next time you film there. PitchClip reads your location only with the app open, when speed is being set up at a field (by you, or by itself before recording), never in the background.

Field locations stay on this phone. They are never sent to the developer, a server, or other phones, not even on the Game feed, and they are not in the log or in a bug report. Up to 60 fields are kept. If you do not allow Location, PitchClip works the same and you set up speed by hand.

Game feed

The Game feed lets other phones running PitchClip near you follow the game. It is on by default. While "Share this phone's game with nearby phones" is on (in Settings), a phone that joins can see this phone's name, the game name, the inning, pitch counts and rest days, the lineup with any names you typed, speeds, tags, plate camera calls, notices about the game and the recording phone, and clips. Each new pitch is also sent ahead to every watching phone as a small copy and saved there, even if nobody opens it. A watching phone can also ask for the full clip. Clips copied to a watching phone stay there.

Each team has a 4 digit team code. When this phone records a game for one of your teams, only phones that know that team's code can join: a parent types it once, and then joins that team's games by itself. The code itself is never shown. Before joining, nearby phones (and any nearby device that looks for them on the Wi-Fi network) can see the team's name, the game's name, this phone's name and how many phones are watching. The recording phone shows who is watching now and can remove any phone from the game. "Ask before a phone joins" also asks you to allow each new phone. A phone recording without a team (for example a quick practice) can be joined by any nearby PitchClip phone, as before.

Team sharing: a phone that joins with the code keeps a copy of the team: the team's name, code and season, player names and numbers, the names of the phones that joined, the team logo, the age group and the usual batting order. It gets changes to these when it joins later games, and changes made on it go back to the team's other phones. For the logo, age group and batting order the latest change wins. Only the team's creator or a coach can set the age group.

Player photos are shared between the team's phones too, but only to fill a blank: a phone sends a player's photo only to a phone that has no photo for that player, and never replaces a photo another phone already has. A photo you removed on this phone is not filled again from another phone. Photos and the logo go only between phones joined to the team with its code, as small pictures with no location or camera details.

Phones of the same team also swap the pitch counts each one counted for the team's players in the last 7 days, with the counting phone's name, so rest dates include games another phone filmed; a phone keeps them for 30 days. Never shared: your own player choice, parent contacts, notes, throwing hands, speed setups, field locations, Coach Credits and your settings.

"Let other phones control the game", also on by default, lets one watching phone at a time, the one in control, change the game the way this phone's own buttons do, including starting recording, the inning, the batter and the pitch count. The phone in control can also ask for a still picture from this phone's camera to set up speed (not while recording). The recording phone can take control back.

Coach at bats: when Coach finishes one of your player's at bats on the recording phone (Every time your player bats), the cards and the summary go as text, with no video or pictures, to the phones watching the game at that moment. A watching phone keeps them only if its own player is that jersey number; other phones drop them.

Plate camera: a second phone behind home plate can link to the recording phone. It joins like any phone on the Game feed, but never gets clip videos or pictures and never controls the game. Its own video stays on that phone, like any clip filmed there. The recording phone sends it clock checks and a short signal each time a pitch starts. From it, its name, clock checks and a small call for each pitch (where the ball crossed the front of the plate, side to side and how high) go to the recording phone, which uses the two phones together to place the pitch and passes each call on with its clip to phones on the Game feed. Like any phone that joins with the team code, it also swaps the team list and pitch counts with the recording phone.

The connection is encrypted and goes directly between the phones over peer-to-peer Wi-Fi or the Wi-Fi network you are on (not Bluetooth), never through the internet or a server. Turn sharing off to keep a game private.

Sharing and saving

Besides the Game feed and Coach, a clip, highlight video or speed card leaves PitchClip only when you share it or save it to Photos. After that it is handled by the app or person you sent it to, under their privacy terms. A highlight video is made on this phone from your clips, with their game sound and no added music. Move to Photos saves the original to your photo library and then removes the video from PitchClip. The pitch details and a still frame stay in PitchClip.

Send to a PitchClip phone makes one file with a clip's video and its details and opens the share sheet. You pick where it goes, usually AirDrop to a phone nearby; it goes through no PitchClip server, and AirDrop delivers it only if the other person accepts it. Videos you import from Photos or Files stay on this phone unless you send one to Coach.

Coach

Coach writes short coaching cards about pitches and swings. It runs only in these ways:

A tap: when you tap Coach on a clip (on the clip's page, in Games & Clips, or from the Coach screen). A tap reviews any clip you pick, as a pitch or a swing, whoever is in it.

Game Review: when you tap Game Review for a game, Coach looks at all of your player's pitches, or all of their swings, in that game together. A Game Review uses Coach Credits. No money is charged in the app.

Every time your player bats: if you turn this on (on the Coach screen; it is off until you do), each pitch of your player's at bats filmed on this phone in a game goes to Coach by itself, with no tap, until you turn it off. After that game, PitchClip also runs that game's batting Game Review by itself. It covers only your own player.

Before anything goes to Coach, Coach says what it sends and names Anthropic, and you agree once. The Every time switch says it too.

Body points: once you have agreed to Coach and set your player, this phone measures body points (where the head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles are, frame by frame) in your player's pitching clips after a game, using Apple's built-in Vision on this phone, never while recording. If no player is set, nothing is measured until you tap Coach on a clip, and then only that clip. The points cover the pitcher and anyone else inside the box around the pitcher. Measuring sends nothing: the points are saved on this phone and go to the server only with a Coach request for that clip.

What is sent for each card: a few seconds of video around one pitch (usually about 2.5 seconds, up to 7 when the phone could not time the pitch), with no sound: the whole picture, or a full-width band with the top and bottom trimmed (usually about 10 MB, at most 15 MB); where that piece sits in the whole picture, and the box you set around the pitcher; your player's jersey number and age; the pitch's measured speed and timing, and the mound distance; your taps on that pitch, such as the call (ball, strike, foul or in play) and how hard the ball was hit; the app version; a random id made for this install of PitchClip, which is not tied to you and is used for a daily limit per phone; and a random id for each review. For a pitching review, also the body points (when there are some), which hand your player throws with (if you set it), whether the fence phone or the plate camera filmed it, the pitch number in the game and the ball and strike count before the pitch. For a batting review, also a close-up of the batter over the same seconds, with no sound (about 4 MB, at most 8 MB), and what the phone measured on that pitch: the pitch's release and plate times, the ball's path, the strike zone guide, the speed setup's plate and rubber marks, whether the bat made contact, and the bat speed. No names are ever sent.

Some pitching cards in a Game Review are made by code from the body points alone: then no video is sent for that pitch, only the points and the details above.

What a Game Review sends: first, a card for each of your player's clips in that game that has none (as above). Then the cards' words and measures, your player's age, the inning or at bat of each card, and the pitch numbers (pitching) or your taps (batting); for batting, also the small still pictures of the batter that Coach made for each card; for pitching, up to 6 still pictures of the release, cut on this phone. Then the clips Coach picks for a close look are sent again as above, with their video. No names are sent.

For an at bat or outing summary, the cards already on this phone are sent again (their words and measures) with your player's age, the pitch counts and the last game's numbers and date, with no video.

The video and still pictures can show other people in the shot, such as the catcher, the umpire, or the other team's pitcher or batter. The batter close-up can show the catcher and the umpire.

Where it goes and who sees it: PitchClip's own server on Google Cloud (Cloud Run, in Dallas, Texas, USA). The server uses Anthropic's Claude to write the cards: it sends Claude still pictures cut from the video and some of the details above, never names. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, what Claude is sent is not used to train models and is deleted within 30 days (longer only if Anthropic must keep it to enforce its usage policy or to comply with the law). It is not sent anywhere else.

How long it is kept: the server deletes the video piece, the batter close-up, the body points and the still pictures as soon as the review finishes. While it runs, it counts each phone's reviews for the day by the random phone id, and starts over each day. For each review it keeps a few log lines in Google Cloud's logs: the random phone id, the review's random id, pitching or batting, your taps, which phone filmed it, the Game Review's random id and the Coach Credits it used, the result, cost, timing, framing and technical notes on how the review ran. Google Cloud also keeps a standard log of each request to the server, with the phone's IP address and the app and iOS versions. These logs hold no video, no pictures and no names, and are deleted after 30 days.

The cards and Game Reviews come back to this iPhone and are saved here with the clips (when Every time your player bats finishes an at bat, its cards also go to the phones watching the game, see Game feed). A batting card's swing measures (timing and body numbers, no video) are also saved here by jersey number, for the player's swing history; the history itself stays on this iPhone. The record of Coach Credits used stays on this iPhone. A notification can tell you a card or a Game Review is ready.

PitchClip also asks the server which Coach version and features it has, for example when you open a card or before body points are measured; that sends nothing about your player or the clip. To keep everything on your phone, don't tap Coach or Game Review and leave Every time your player bats off. Coach sends nothing otherwise.

Feedback and bug reports

Send feedback (Settings, Help, privacy and feedback) opens an email to the developer in your mail app. It includes your message, the app version, phone model, iOS version and the diagnostic log as an attachment. You can read or remove anything before sending. The developer also sees your email address, because the email comes from you.

Send a bug report (Settings) makes one file and opens the share sheet. It goes only to the person or app you pick. It holds the app version, phone model, iOS version, time zone, your PitchClip settings and whether notifications are on, the team list with its team code and the names and ids of the phones that joined, the lineup with any names you typed, game and opponent names, the current pitch list, the details of clips from the last 3 days (no video), free storage space and recent log lines, and iOS's crash and performance reports for PitchClip (no video). It never holds field locations or photos.

The developer uses what you send only to answer you and fix problems, keeps it only as long as that takes, never sells or shares it, and deletes it when you ask.

Purchases

PitchClip Premium is sold by Apple through the App Store. Apple handles payment and your Apple Account. PitchClip only asks the App Store whether Premium is active on this iPhone. The developer never receives your name, email address or payment details. Apple's privacy policy covers purchases. Coach Credits are not sold in the app today; nothing about them involves money or payment details.

Children

PitchClip is made for parents and coaches, not for children to use on their own. Because it films youth baseball, clips show children, and parents can type players' names and pick player photos.

That information stays on the parent's iPhone unless the parent chooses to send it. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. A child's name, photo or video reaches someone else only when a parent shares a clip, uses the Game feed or joins a team with its code, sends feedback or a bug report, or uses Coach. When a parent uses Coach (a tap on a clip, a Game Review, or Every time your player bats turned on), a few seconds of the child's video (for batting, also a close-up of the batter), body points measured on the phone, still pictures, the jersey number, age and the other details listed under Coach (never a name) go to PitchClip's server, which uses Anthropic's Claude to write the cards. The server deletes the video, pictures and body points as soon as the review finishes.

PitchClip is intended for adults, and the developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If a child's information reaches the developer in feedback or a bug report, it is used only to fix the problem and is deleted on request. Parents can contact the developer at any time about their child's information.

Please think of other families when you share: clips can show other players, the Game feed shows the lineup to nearby PitchClip phones, team sharing passes player photos to the team's other phones, and a Coach video piece can show other players and the umpire.

Permissions

Camera and microphone: to film pitches with game sound, from the fence or from behind home plate. The camera can also take a photo of a lineup card when you choose to. Neither is used for anything else. Photos: to save clips you choose and, if you allow it, to read your newest screenshot for a lineup and to check that a moved clip arrived. Picking a player photo, a team logo or one screenshot needs no Photos permission, because PitchClip sees only the picture you pick. Player photos and the logo are saved without their location or camera details. Local network: to find nearby phones for the Game feed and to link a plate camera. Notifications: alerts made on this phone, such as when recording stops or a Coach card or Game Review is ready; PitchClip uses no push notification server. Location (While Using the App): only if you allow it after saving a speed setup, to remember each field's setup so speed is ready by itself there next time. It stays on this phone and is never sent. You can change each permission in the iPhone Settings app.

Deleting your data

A deleted clip stays in Recently deleted for 7 days, then goes away on its own. To remove it sooner, open Settings, Storage and empty Recently deleted. A clip's Coach card and its saved swing measures are deleted with the clip, and Undo or Recently deleted brings them back.

Settings, Delete all clips on this phone removes every clip with its Coach cards, Game Reviews, Coach at bat summaries, body points and swing measures, and empties Recently deleted. Pitch counts, the team list, player photos, the team logo, the numbers from past Coach summaries, the record of Coach Credits, remembered fields, settings and the log stay until you delete the app.

Remove a player's photo on that player's page; other phones of the team keep the copy they already have. Removing the team logo removes it on the team's other phones too. Moving a player to Former players keeps the player and their clips on this phone. To remove a team's players from this phone, delete the team in My team; its games and clips stay. To stop PitchClip from using or saving field locations, turn Location off for PitchClip in the iPhone Settings app.

Deleting PitchClip removes everything it stored on this iPhone, including remembered fields. Copies in an iPhone backup stay until that backup is replaced or deleted. Clips you saved to Photos or shared, and the clips, Coach at bats, team lists, logos, player photos and pitch counts copied to other phones through the Game feed, stay where they are. To have the developer delete anything you emailed, write to the address below.

Coach keeps no video, pictures or body points after a review: the server deletes them as soon as the review finishes. Its logs hold no video, no pictures and no names and are deleted after 30 days.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will appear in the app and on the web page for this policy, with a new effective date.
